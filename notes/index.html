The Kummer–Dedekind Theorem

How does a prime p factor in the ring of integers of a number field? The Kummer–Dedekind theorem reduces the question to factoring a polynomial modulo p, and the same proof describes the fibers of a plane curve projected onto a line. The note splits the theorem into three parts according to the hypotheses each one needs, and works through number field examples, curve examples, Dedekind’s example of a prime dividing the index, and why primes dividing the discriminant ramify.

How Polynomials Factor Modulo Primes

An irreducible polynomial with integer coefficients usually factors modulo p, and how it factors changes with p. Starting from data for five quartics, the note shows that the factorization type of f modulo p is the cycle type of a Frobenius element of the Galois group, then proves the Frobenius density theorem: the proportion of primes giving each factorization type equals the proportion of elements of the Galois group with that cycle type. The proof needs only Kronecker’s average, Burnside’s lemma, and some linear algebra.

Ultraproducts and Łoś’s Theorem

Ultrafilters, ultraproducts, and Łoś’s theorem, which says that a first-order sentence holds in an ultraproduct exactly when it holds in almost every factor. Applications include compactness, nonstandard models, moving between characteristic p and characteristic 0, and the theorems of Ax–Grothendieck and Ax–Kochen, followed by some pitfalls and suggestions for further reading.

Simplicity of the Projective Special Linear Groups

A proof that PSLn(F) is simple for every field F and every n ≥ 2, apart from PSL2(𝔽2) and PSL2(𝔽3), using Iwasawa’s criterion for doubly transitive group actions. This note is a fork of Keith Conrad’s expository note Simplicity of PSLn(F): the case n = 2 has new proofs, and the case n > 2 largely follows Conrad’s exposition.

Galois’s Exceptional Primes 5, 7, and 11

In his letter to Chevalier on the night before his duel, Galois stated a result that in modern terms says: for a prime p ≥ 5, the group PSL2(𝔽p) has a subgroup of index p only when p is 5, 7, or 11. The note proves this with a Burnside count on the projective line over 𝔽p2, constructs the exceptional subgroups A4, S4, and A5 from one pair of integer matrices, and describes the actions on p points: A5 on five letters, the Fano plane, and the Paley biplane.

The Classifying Topos of Groups: a Case Study

A classifying topos is to a theory what a polynomial ring is to a system of unknowns: a universe containing a generic model, from which every model anywhere is obtained by substitution. After introducing geometric theories, their models in a topos, and the classifying topos, the note works out the theory of groups in detail. Its classifying topos is the category of functors from finitely presented groups to sets, the generic group is the forgetful functor, and substitution is evaluation, carried out by a filtered colimit for an arbitrary group and by a coend for a group object in any topos.

The Riemann Hypothesis in Brief

Panel discussion on the Riemann Hypothesis, Vassar College Department of Mathematics and Statistics · November 2026

In 1859 Bernhard Riemann published a nine-page paper on the distribution of the prime numbers, and in it a remark he could not prove. This short talk explains what the Riemann Hypothesis says, how the zeros of the zeta function control the primes, and the two proofs announced on November 20, 2026.

Down the Rabbit Hole: An Introduction to p-adic Numbers

Math Day keynote address, Colorado State University · November 2026

It’s a curious fact that 1 = 0.999… where the 9s repeat forever to the right. An even curiouser fact is that −1 = …999 where the 9s repeat forever to the left. Now that may seem crazy, but in this talk I will explain the twisted logic behind it which leads us down a rabbit hole to the strange world of p-adic numbers.

Interactive

Interactive tools and visualizations, mostly built for my courses. Each one runs in the browser.

Portalz

Walk around inside 3-manifolds built as branched covers. Simple rooms are stitched together along invisible membranes and explored seamlessly in first person. In the knot hall a trefoil, figure-eight knot, or Hopf link floats overhead, and looking through the knot shows you the other sheets of the cover, like the colored rooms of the S3 cover of the trefoil pictured here.

Timeline of Mathematics

A tool for visualizing the history of mathematics. Choose mathematicians and events from a list to add them to the timeline, then click any of them for a short description and an image. I made it to give historical context in my classes, so the current selection reflects the occasions where I have used it.

Open GitHub

Row Reducer

Practice row reduction one elementary row operation at a time, with exact rational arithmetic. Start from a matrix or a linear system, watch each operation animate, and see when you reach row echelon and reduced row echelon form. Work can be shared by link or exported to LaTeX. Inspired by the row reduction demo in Margalit and Rabinoff’s Interactive Linear Algebra.

See what a matrix does to space. Enter a 2 × 2, 2 × 3, 3 × 2, or 3 × 3 matrix and watch it carry a figure, the unit circle or sphere, and a lattice from the domain to the codomain, drawn side by side in two or three dimensions.

The Group Determinant

Math 301 Representation Theory · Fall 2026

Explore Frobenius’s group determinant. Choose a finite group to see its presentation, its multiplication table, the matrix (xgh), and the factorization of det(xgh) into irreducible polynomials: one factor for each irreducible representation, raised to the power of its degree.
